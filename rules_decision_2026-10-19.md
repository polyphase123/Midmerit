# Decision on the GAIR: GAT Price structure and open comments (19 October 2026)

Based on all stakeholder comments (New Comments, old comments and the GAIR Comments Matrix), Ma'am Sharon's 29 and Antonette's 3 comments on the draft, DOE DC2026-06-0013, and the DOE Terms of Reference (Annex B).

## 1. GAT Price structure: decided

**GATP = CRF + OM. The CLLC moves out of the GAT Price into the Fuel Cost as a capped component: FC = FCC + CLLC.**

| Option | Verdict | Main reason |
|---|---|---|
| A. GATP = CRF + OM + CLLC (previous draft) | Rejected | Logistics costs differ by fuel source, so bids were not comparable (Sharon's "ISSUE ON CLLC"). Lot 1 non-mid-merit energy is paid Fuel Cost only (TOR 4.2.2, 17.1.2), so logistics costs on that energy were never recovered. |
| B. GATP = CRF only; O&M and CLLC into the FC (earlier 19 Oct revision) | Rejected | DC Secs. 4 and 7.2 define the FCPT and FC as gas costs, so O&M has no DOE basis there. DC 6.1(a) puts "operating" costs in the GAT methodology. It also removes O&M from competition and adds a yearly true-up and related-party audits. |
| **C. GATP = CRF + OM; CLLC into the FC with caps** | **Adopted** | Logistics caps sit in the Gas Price Benchmark (DC Sec. 4, 18.2.2–18.2.3). Restores one GAT Price for all fuel sources (Sec. 10). Recovers logistics costs on non-mid-merit energy. Asked for by Aboitiz (rows 162, 218) and NAC. |
| D. Fuel-inclusive GAT Price (White Paper) | Rejected | DC 7.2 and TOR 4.2/17 pay the GAP and the Fuel Cost separately. |

**Safeguards kept:**
- per-component caps (DC 18.2.2; PMCJ, MERALCO);
- lower-of rule for a bidder-disclosed CLLC value (MERALCO);
- above-cap approval within 60 days on a least-cost test (SPPC, MERALCO, Sharon);
- per-Fuel-Source-Category caps (Tokyo Gas, SPPC);
- BOG excluded (Sharon);
- Section 25 audit;
- no return or mark-up through the CLLC (Sec. 28).

**Not adopted:**
- uncapped audit-only recovery (Aboitiz);
- capacity-based recovery of fixed terminal charges (Tokyo Gas), because Sec. 28 and the TOR pay per kWh delivered;
- a fuel-inclusive GAT Price (White Paper);
- post-COD indexation (PCG, NAC), because Sec. 8.9 provides none.

**Consumer effect.** Consumers pay actual logistics costs up to the caps, instead of an allowance priced into every bid. The Winning Bidder still carries cost above the caps and volume risk.

## 3. Ma'am Sharon's comments

All 29 were already answered or implemented in the draft. The decision above supersedes two earlier answers:
- **"ISSUE ON CLLC"** was earlier fixed with a separate GAT Price per Fuel Source Category. It is now resolved by moving the CLLC into the Fuel Cost, with per-category caps.
- **"How to ensure least-cost CLLC"** is answered by the safeguards in section 1.
